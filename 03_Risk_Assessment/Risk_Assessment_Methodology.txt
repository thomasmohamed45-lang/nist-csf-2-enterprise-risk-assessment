RISK ASSESSMENT METHODOLOGY
PINE RIDGE LOGISTICS, INC.
NIST CSF 2.0 ENTERPRISE CYBERSECURITY RISK ASSESSMENT

1. PURPOSE

This document establishes the methodology used to identify, analyze, evaluate, and prioritize cybersecurity risks within the simulated Pine Ridge Logistics environment.

The methodology is informed by NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1 and organized using the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0.

The numerical scoring model below is a project-defined approach. It is not a scoring formula prescribed by NIST.

2. RISK ASSESSMENT PROCESS

The assessment follows these activities:

1. Identify organizational assets and business processes.
2. Identify relevant threat events.
3. Identify vulnerabilities and predisposing conditions.
4. Estimate the likelihood of each threat event.
5. Estimate the potential business impact.
6. Determine inherent risk.
7. Identify existing or assumed security controls.
8. Determine residual risk after considering controls.
9. Recommend risk treatment actions.
10. Prioritize remediation based on risk exposure.

3. LIKELIHOOD SCALE

Likelihood measures how plausible it is that a threat event could occur under the assessed conditions.

1 - RARE
The event is highly unlikely. Significant barriers or effective safeguards may make occurrence improbable.

2 - UNLIKELY
The event is possible but not expected under normal conditions.

3 - POSSIBLE
The event could occur. Relevant threats or weaknesses may exist.

4 - LIKELY
The event is expected to occur in some circumstances. Relevant exposure or weaknesses are significant.

5 - ALMOST CERTAIN
The event is highly expected or may occur frequently due to substantial exposure or known recurring conditions.

4. IMPACT SCALE

Impact measures the potential consequences if a threat event occurs.

Impact considers:
- Confidentiality: Unauthorized disclosure of information.
- Integrity: Unauthorized modification or destruction of information.
- Availability: Disruption or loss of access to systems and services.
- Financial losses.
- Operational disruption.
- Legal or regulatory consequences.
- Reputational damage.
- Potential safety consequences where applicable.

1 - NEGLIGIBLE
Minimal disruption, limited data exposure, and negligible financial or operational consequences.

2 - MINOR
Limited disruption affecting a small number of users or a noncritical business process.

3 - MODERATE
Noticeable disruption to a business function, recoverable data exposure, or meaningful financial impact.

4 - MAJOR
Significant operational disruption, sensitive information exposure, substantial financial loss, or serious compliance consequences.

5 - SEVERE
Organization-wide disruption, compromise of highly sensitive information, major financial damage, prolonged service outage, or potentially serious safety consequences.

5. RISK SCORING FORMULA

Risk Score = Likelihood x Impact

Both likelihood and impact are rated on a scale from 1 through 5.

The resulting numerical score ranges from 1 through 25.

6. RISK RATING THRESHOLDS

1-4: LOW
Manage through routine security practices and periodic monitoring.

5-9: MODERATE
Document the risk and establish appropriate mitigation or monitoring activities.

10-16: HIGH
Prioritize risk treatment and assign an accountable owner and target completion date.

17-25: CRITICAL
Escalate to organizational leadership for urgent review and treatment decisions.

7. INHERENT RISK

Inherent risk represents the estimated risk before considering the effect of existing security controls.

Inherent Risk Score = Inherent Likelihood x Inherent Impact

8. RESIDUAL RISK

Residual risk represents the remaining risk after considering the effectiveness of existing or assumed security controls.

Residual Risk Score = Residual Likelihood x Residual Impact

Residual scores must be supported by documented control assumptions. Controls will not automatically reduce risk merely because they are listed.

9. RISK TREATMENT OPTIONS

ACCEPT
Management formally acknowledges and accepts the risk.

MITIGATE
Implement safeguards to reduce likelihood, impact, or both.

TRANSFER
Shift or share some financial or contractual consequences through mechanisms such as insurance or vendor agreements. Accountability for the risk may remain.

AVOID
Discontinue or change the activity that creates the exposure.

10. RISK OWNERSHIP

Each identified risk will have:
- A unique risk identifier.
- An affected asset or business process.
- A documented threat event.
- An identified vulnerability or predisposing condition.
- Likelihood and impact ratings.
- An inherent risk score and rating.
- Existing or assumed control information.
- A residual risk score and rating.
- A recommended treatment strategy.
- An accountable risk owner.
- A target remediation date.

11. ASSESSMENT ASSUMPTIONS AND LIMITATIONS

Pine Ridge Logistics is fictional. No actual systems, vulnerabilities, incidents, or security controls have been independently verified.

Risk ratings and control descriptions will be identified as simulated assessment assumptions.

The scoring model provides a consistent prioritization method for this portfolio exercise. Numerical scores do not represent objectively measured probabilities or guaranteed financial losses.
